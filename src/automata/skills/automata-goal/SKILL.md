# Automata Goal

Clarify what success means, not just which method or artifact was requested.
Reuse clear intent; small requests need no goal interview or formal document.

1. **Understand the outcome.** Separate purpose, outcome, proposed method,
   constraints and assumptions. Include working preferences that affect success,
   without inventing motives or treating inferred preferences as confirmed.
   Identify essential constraints, acceptable tradeoffs and outcome-level evidence;
   concrete checks belong with the workplan.

2. **Resolve meaningful uncertainty.** Ask the smallest question that unlocks work
   when ambiguity or conflicting priorities changes the next decision. Otherwise
   use judgment within established intent. An alternative is not permission to
   replace the user's goal or ignore a constraint.

3. **Keep work aligned.** Carry relevant priorities and constraints into plans,
   assignments and reviews using existing state, not a separate goal registry.
   Adapt methods as evidence changes; surface infeasibility rather than silently
   lowering standards. Revisit affected assumptions at useful boundaries, not
   repeatedly. Align before changing the goal, material constraints or scope.
   Changing methods or team arrangements need not change the goal.

4. **Preserve direction only when wanted.** Reuse approved records and locations;
   agree on new placement before writing. `goal/main.md` and `goal/sub-goals/` are
   options, not defaults. Save enduring outcomes and priorities, not commands,
   current status or task phases. A reference in `AGENTS.md` needs its own confirmed
   scope and wording; documenting a goal does not authorize instruction edits.

Goal reasoning owns outcomes and priorities. Planning owns the route and checks;
delegation owns assignments and coordination; tracking owns task status. Clarification alone grants
no authority to implement, delegate or publish.
